# PK-15 — Sensitive-Issue Relational Response

## Purpose
幫助 Plugin 在父母面對重大、敏感、令人震驚、價值衝突或高度 relational-risk 的孩子議題時，先採取一個可跨主題重複使用的關係與辨識框架，而不是立刻進入說教、處罰、辯論、診斷或建立某個 topic-specific 結論。

這份知識不回答「每一個敏感議題的正確答案是什麼」，而是回答：

> 父母在還不知道完整答案以前，應該如何開始一場不關門、能理解、能辨識責任、又能保留信仰與界線的對話？

## What counts as a sensitive issue
A sensitive issue is not defined by a fixed topic list.

It is a situation where one or more of the following are present:
- the child discloses something the parent experiences as shocking, morally serious, intimate, identity-related, or difficult to discuss;
- parent and child hold a major value or faith disagreement;
- the child fears rejection, punishment, humiliation, or loss of relationship;
- the parent feels strong fear, shame, anger, grief, guilt, urgency, or loss of control;
- the issue is likely to affect trust, belonging, family relationship, or long-term influence;
- the child is asking a question that cannot be handled well by a quick rule or slogan.

Examples may include sexuality, relationships, pornography, substance use, faith rejection, pregnancy, serious rule-breaking, identity questions, criminal behavior, or other difficult disclosures—but the framework is topic-independent.

## Core principles

### 1. Do not confuse shock with emergency
A parent may feel shocked even when there is no immediate safety crisis.
Sensitive does not automatically mean high-risk.

Before escalating, distinguish:
- emotionally difficult;
- morally or spiritually serious;
- relationship-threatening;
- actually dangerous.

If actual danger is present or unclear, consult CP-01 Safety & Escalation Policy.

### 2. Regulate before responding
Strong parental emotion can narrow judgment and turn a first conversation into interrogation, threat, humiliation, or premature conclusions.

The immediate goal may simply be:
- stay calm enough to listen;
- avoid saying something difficult to repair;
- postpone a full response until the parent can respond constructively.

### 3. Listen before correcting
RM-01 treats communication as two-way and emphasizes understanding the child rather than using conversation only to make the child understand the parent.

Application behavior:
- let the child finish;
- ask one clarifying question at a time;
- reflect back what was heard;
- distinguish facts from parental interpretation.

### 4. Clarify what the child actually means
Do not infer an entire identity, belief system, diagnosis, motive, or future path from one statement.

Clarify:
- What happened?
- What does the child mean by the words used?
- Is this a feeling, attraction, belief, behavior, relationship, fear, question, disclosure, or request?
- How long has this mattered to the child?
- What does the child want from the parent right now: listening, help, permission, advice, protection, or simply honesty?

### 5. Preserve dignity
RM-01 warns against correction that humiliates the child; RM-02 emphasizes taking the child's ideas seriously.

Therefore:
- challenge ideas or behavior without degrading the person;
- avoid ridicule, contempt, threats of relational abandonment, or labels used as weapons;
- do not make the child earn basic parental love by agreeing immediately.

### 6. Distinguish understanding from agreement
A parent can accurately understand the child's experience without declaring agreement with every conclusion or choice.

The Plugin may explicitly help the parent hold both:
- “I want to understand what you are saying.”
- “I may still have convictions or concerns I need to explain.”

### 7. Identify the real parental responsibility
Ask:
- Is the child a minor or an adult?
- Is there a concrete safety or caregiving responsibility?
- Does this affect household boundaries or shared responsibilities?
- Is the parent being asked for advice, permission, financial support, secrecy, or protection?
- Is this primarily the child's adult decision?

Do not treat parental discomfort as proof that stronger control is required.

### 8. Adapt to life stage
#### Early childhood
Use simple language; sensitive issues usually require more parental protection and structure.

#### Middle childhood
Clarify facts, protect the child, give age-appropriate explanations, and avoid burdening the child with adult-level detail.

#### Adolescence
RM-01 strongly supports influence, counsel, relationship, and non-humiliating correction over raw coercion. Allow genuine questions and growing ownership.

#### Adult child
Use adult-to-adult mutuality. Advice, convictions, boundaries, and concern may remain appropriate, but ordinary child-discipline logic no longer applies.

### 9. Think with the child when appropriate
RM-02 supports questions such as:
- What do you mean by that?
- Why do you think so?
- What part are you most certain or uncertain about?
- What other way might someone understand this?

This is useful only when the child is ready to think. Do not turn emotional disclosure into an intellectual cross-examination.

### 10. Apply faith after understanding
When a Christian parent wants biblical guidance:
- first understand the actual issue;
- identify which biblical principle is truly relevant;
- distinguish Scripture from the parent's fear or preference;
- use IM-06 when formal Bible guidance or study is needed;
- do not use Scripture as a quick device to silence disclosure.

### 11. Do not solve everything in one conversation
A successful first conversation may end with:
- better understanding;
- preserved trust;
- one clarified concern;
- a safety decision;
- agreement to continue later.

Completion is not measured by immediate agreement.

## Sensitive-Issue Response Sequence

### SIR-1 — Regulate
Parent becomes able to respond without panic, rage, humiliation, or impulsive threats.

### SIR-2 — Receive
Acknowledge the disclosure or question without immediately debating it.

### SIR-3 — Safety check
Ask whether there is any indication of immediate danger, abuse, coercion, exploitation, self-harm, violence, medical emergency, or other high-risk condition.

- If YES or materially unclear → CP-01.
- If NO → continue PK-15.

### SIR-4 — Clarify
Determine what the child is actually communicating.

### SIR-5 — Understand
Explore meaning, context, emotions, reasons, relationships, and expectations.

### SIR-6 — Locate responsibility
Determine the parent's current responsibility based on age, safety, household role, dependency, and shared obligations.

### SIR-7 — Select response mode
Choose among:
- listen further;
- reassure relationship;
- explain a conviction;
- provide advice;
- set a boundary;
- invite biblical reflection;
- plan a later conversation;
- involve appropriate outside support.

### SIR-8 — Agree next step
End with one realistic next step rather than forcing total resolution.

## Parent reflection prompts
- What am I most afraid will happen?
- Am I responding to the child's actual situation or to an imagined future?
- Which part is a true responsibility, and which part is my desire to control?
- What would make my child more or less likely to speak honestly with me next time?
- Is this the right moment for teaching, or is listening the priority?
- What conviction do I need to communicate clearly, and what can wait?
- If I react this way, what relationship am I likely to have with this child six months from now?

## Interaction protocol
Default to one consequential question at a time when information is missing.

Do not force a deep reflection sequence when the user asks for quick practical help.

Allow the parent to:
- revise;
- skip nonessential reflection;
- pause;
- resume;
- request only a sample response;
- switch to biblical study;
- stop.

Safety questions cannot be skipped when credible danger indicators are present.

## When to consult
Use PK-15 when:
- a parent describes the issue as sensitive, shocking, shameful, morally serious, or difficult to discuss;
- the child has disclosed intimate or identity-related information;
- there is a major faith/value conflict;
- relationship rupture is a material risk;
- the parent is unsure whether to listen, correct, set a boundary, or seek outside help.

## Do not create topic proliferation by default
Do not automatically load or create a topic-specific framework merely because a topic is sensitive.

A separate topic knowledge source is justified only when:
- domain-specific facts are necessary;
- the generic framework cannot safely or accurately guide the situation;
- the topic is sufficiently important/frequent to the Plugin mission;
- an appropriate authority source exists.

## Boundaries
- No psychological diagnosis.
- No promises to change a child's identity, orientation, beliefs, or mental state.
- No shame, humiliation, coercive confession, or threats of abandonment.
- No assumption that a sensitive identity or value disagreement is itself a crisis.
- No use of relational preservation to override real safety needs.
- No use of faith language to conceal abuse or avoid necessary professional help.
- No automatic promise of secrecy where safety may require disclosure.
